FileConcat reads every document in the browser tab it was dropped into. Nothing is uploaded and
no GPU is used. We ran public extraction benchmarks through that tab to see what the trade costs.

<Contents />

## How good is text extraction in the browser?

Well enough for most of what people drop, and measurably short on two things: scanned PDFs and
speech that is not English.

| File type | How it reads in the tab | What falls short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PDF with a text layer | level with the best readers that need no GPU | tables, tiny print, math |
| Scanned PDF | readable, well behind GPU readers | math, old typewritten pages |
| Saved web page | close to the best server tools | pages saved by Firefox |
| Word, Excel, PowerPoint, RTF | agrees with Apache Tika as much as two good readers do | pptx, and workbooks with macros |
| Ebook (epub, mobi, azw3) | near word for word | DRM-locked store books |
| Archive (zip, tar, gz, 7z) | files come back exact | an archive inside an archive |
| Video with a subtitle track | word for word, instantly | nothing measured |
| Video speech, English | about 1 word in 8 wrong | names and rare words |
| Video speech, Turkish | about 1 word in 3 wrong | everything, it is the weakest row |

The pattern under the table is simple. Where the file stores its own text, as a text layer, a
subtitle track or an archive member, the browser reads it as well as anything on a server. Where
the text has to be recognised from pixels or sound, the size of the model decides it, and the
models that fit in a tab are small.

<Stopper value="40.0 vs 83.1" source="olmOCR-bench, 1,403 pages / production build / 2026-10-07">

  math, which no reader without a vision model writes at all.

## Where the browser still loses

Three losses are big enough to plan around.

1. **Scanned pages and math.** A page with no text layer is read by an OCR model small enough to run
   on a laptop without a graphics card. The GPU readers at the top of the PDF benchmark are vision
   models a hundred times larger. For a stack of scans you need exact, they are the better tool.
2. **Web pages saved by Firefox.** Chrome marks a saved page with the address it came from, and
   that mark is how the tool knows the file is a page to read rather than HTML source to keep. A
   page saved by Firefox carries no mark and is bundled as source code, markup and all.
3. **Speech in languages other than English.** English is heard by a model built for it. Every other
   language goes through Whisper small, which is a quarter of a gigabyte to download and got about
   a third of the Turkish words wrong.

Measuring this found one bug in our tool, now fixed: the first transcription in a session ignored
the browser's language and listened for English.

## What to do with your own files

Drop them. If a file is a scan, a recording or a page from Firefox, open the result and read the
first lines before you paste the bundle, because those are the three places the text is a guess.

- For a scan you need exact, run it through a GPU reader first and drop the text it returns.
- For a Firefox page, save it again from Chrome, or paste the article into a text file.
- For a recording with subtitles, keep the subtitle track in the file: it is read exactly and
  costs nothing.

Every format the tab reads, and how, is listed on [the formats page](/docs/formats).

## What nobody had measured yet

Every published extraction benchmark we found runs its readers natively, on a server or a
workstation, and most are written by the vendor of the tool that wins them. We looked for a
measurement of extraction inside a browser tab, for any format, and found none. The closest are
browser ports of a reader published with a demo and no numbers.

So the question here is one: how close does extraction inside a browser tab, with no GPU and
nothing uploaded, come to the best published readers, format by format?

The sources contradict each other in the place that matters most here. LiteParse, the PDF reader
FileConcat now uses, scores 39.6 on olmOCR-bench in its own README and 20.4 in Marker's, on the
same benchmark with OCR off in both. Three of the multi-format benchmarks rank their own vendor's
tool first. We ran every reader on our side under the same scorer, so the gap between two rows of
our tables is not a gap between two harnesses.

## How we ran it

Every file was dropped into the web app's production build in headless Chromium, the way a
visitor drops it, and the text was taken from the downloaded Plain bundle. Nothing reached a
server. The scorers are each benchmark's own.

The browser was Chromium with an English or Turkish language setting, matching each video's
speech, since that setting is what picks the speech and OCR model. Documents were dropped in
batches of up to 20 from one folder. A PDF that came back without text was dropped again on its
own, because the tool reads at most three scans per drop by itself. Archives and videos went one
per drop.

## PDF: 1,403 pages of olmOCR-bench

olmOCR-bench is 1,403 single PDF pages with 8,413 pass or fail tests: a sentence that must appear,
a running header that must not, two sentences that must come in order, a table cell that must sit
under the right heading, an equation that must render. The score is the mean over its eight parts.

Two parts ask for equations written as LaTeX. A text layer holds the glyphs of an equation, not
its structure, so every reader without a vision model scores zero on them, and they are a quarter
of the score before a page is read. On the other six parts the tab sits between 0.1 and 6.5
points of LiteParse, ahead on tiny print and on scans.

Scans are the weak row. A reader that returns nothing for a scanned page passes 13.3% of the
old-scan tests, because the tests that check something is absent pass on an empty page. The tab's
OCR lifts that to 17.3%: it reads the page, but rarely exactly enough to match the benchmark's
test sentences. The FileConcat 2026-10-03 column is the reader the product used before it
switched to LiteParse on 2026-10-05.

## Saved web pages: 181 articles

A page saved from Chrome is read the way Firefox's reader view reads it: Mozilla Readability picks
the article, and the tool turns it into Markdown. The benchmark scores the article text only, so
we score three versions of the same output.

The as-written row is lower for reasons a model does not mind: the bundle adds the page title and
the address it was saved from, and keeps each link's target, which the benchmark counts as words
that are not article. The last row is the real loss. Without the mark, the file is treated as
HTML source and bundled verbatim.

## Office files against Apache Tika

Office formats have no public answer key, so a mature reader stands in for one: Apache Tika 3.3.2,
built on Apache POI. The score is agreement with Tika, word 4-gram F1, and between two good
readers it tops out around 0.7 to 0.8, because Tika also reads headers, comments and embedded
files that other readers leave out.

Two sets were used. The first is 587 modern Office files converted to the old formats by
LibreOffice, scored against Tika on the original, with Tika on the converted copy as the ceiling.
The second is Apache POI's own test files, the files POI is debugged against.

## Ebooks, archives and video

**Ebooks.** 15 Project Gutenberg books in five languages, each as epub, mobi and azw3, scored
against Gutenberg's own plain text: epub 0.988, mobi 0.996, azw3 0.996, no failures.

**Archives.** 26 archives built from known files, one variant per case readers get wrong: zip
compression methods past deflate, zip64, non-ASCII names with and without the UTF-8 flag, four tar
dialects, gz, bz2 and xz, and 7z across its methods.

| Reader | Archives with every text file exact |
| --- | --- |
| FileConcat, in the tab, 2026-10-07 | 25 of 26 |
| 7-Zip wasm, Node, 2026-10-03 | 24 of 26 |
| libarchive wasm, Node, 2026-10-03 | 21 of 26 |
| FileConcat before 2026-10-05 | 8 of 26 |

The one miss is a zip inside a zip: the inner archive is not opened. On libarchive's own rar and
7z test files, which include damaged and encrypted ones, all 155 finished without a crash or a
hang, and 59 gave back text.

**Video.** 12 videos in five variants: three with a subtitle track (mp4, mkv, webm), one with
neither track nor captions, one with captions burned into the picture. Speech is LibriSpeech and
FLEURS recordings with known transcripts, over slides of known text.

| What was read | English (10 videos) | Turkish (2 videos) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| subtitle track, all three containers | 0% word error | 0% word error |
| speech, no track | 12.7% word error | 32.4% word error |
| on-screen text, F1 | 0.827 | 0.818 |
| on-screen text with burned-in captions, F1 | 0.739 | 0.779 |

English speech runs on Moonshine base, a 66 MB model. Its published error on LibriSpeech is about
3% on clean speech and 8% on noisy speech, read in short clips. Ours is a minute or two
of audio pulled from a compressed video on one CPU thread. Turkish runs on Whisper small, 244 MB.

## What we fixed while measuring

The reading dialog that starts a transcription took its language from the last reading, and fell
back to English when there was none. A recording is never read on its own, so its first reading
always started there, and a Turkish talk went to the English-only model, which returned nothing.
It now falls back to the browser's language. The Turkish rows above are from the fixed build. The
English rows did not change, and were not rerun.

## Limitations

- **One browser.** Chromium only. Firefox and Safari decode audio and video differently and were
  not run.
- **Not a laptop.** Timing was taken on a desktop, so no timing figure is published here. Speed
  on a typical laptop is slower.
- **Small speech samples.** Turkish is two videos. Its 32.4% is a direction, not a figure to
  compare against a published leaderboard.
- **Agreement is not truth.** The Office scores measure agreement with Tika, which keeps headers,
  footers and comments some readers drop on purpose. One producer, LibreOffice, wrote every
  converted file.
- **Archives favour 7-Zip.** The 7z variants were written by 7-Zip, which the tab reads them with.
  rar has no keyed set at all, since only WinRAR writes it.
- **Weak and small:** a Word 6 file scored 0, since only Word 97 and later is read, and two dotx
  templates scored 0.009.
- **Not measured:** layout beyond the benchmarks' own tests, email, encrypted files.

## Reproduce it

Build the web app and serve it, then drop a corpus through it.

```bash
pnpm build
cd apps/web && pnpm vite preview --port 4719 --strictPort
node packages/cli/scripts/measure-live-build.mjs http://localhost:4719/ <corpus dir> out.json
node packages/cli/scripts/measure-live-build.mjs http://localhost:4719/ <pdfs dir> out.json --olmocr <candidate dir>
node packages/cli/scripts/measure-live-build.mjs http://localhost:4719/ <video dir> out.json --transcribe
```

Score with `packages/cli/scripts/score-against-reference.py` (Office, ebooks),
`score-video.py` (video), olmOCR-bench's `python -m olmocr.bench.benchmark` at commit f7cfe4c
(PDF) and the article benchmark's `evaluate.py` (web pages). The archive and video test sets are
written by `build-archive-corpus.py` and `build-video-corpus.py` in the same folder, with a fixed
seed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a browser extract text from a PDF without uploading it?**
Yes. FileConcat reads the text layer in the tab and runs OCR on pages that have none. On 1,403
benchmark pages it scored 40.0, against 38.9 for LiteParse run alone and 82.4 for olmOCR 2 on a
GPU.

**How accurate is in-browser OCR?**
Good enough to search and summarise a scan, not good enough for math or degraded typewritten
pages. The GPU readers that lead the benchmark are far larger than anything a tab can load.

**Can I get text out of a saved web page?**
Yes, if it was saved from Chrome. On 181 benchmark pages the article text matched at 0.937 F1,
one point under Mozilla Readability. A page saved by Firefox is bundled as HTML source.

**Does it read old Word, Excel and PowerPoint files?**
doc, xls and ppt from Word 97 onward, plus rtf. Agreement with Apache Tika was 0.63 to 0.79, about
what two good readers reach. Word 6 and older is not read.

**Can it transcribe a video?**
On request. A subtitle track is read exactly and at once. Without one, speech is transcribed in
the tab after a one-time model download: 66 MB for English, 244 MB for any other language.
