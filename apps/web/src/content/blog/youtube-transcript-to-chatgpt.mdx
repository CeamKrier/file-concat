Take a forty-minute talk. As text you can search it, quote it,
skim the middle and ask a question of it. As video you have to watch it, even
at double speed.

So the useful move is to get the words out first and put the video back only if you need
it. For a video with captions, the transcript sits behind a Show transcript button in the description. That is why this is more awkward than it should be.

## What people do today

The manual route works and is worth knowing. Open the video, expand the description,
click **Show transcript**, then select the transcript panel and copy it.

What you get back is a column of timestamps with a line of speech beside each one, with
nothing to say which video it came from:

work by hand for one video, without its title or description.

## Take the transcript and the description together

FileConcat Clipper is a browser side panel that reads a watch page and hands you the
video's description and its transcript as one Markdown file.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the panel on the video">
    Click the toolbar icon. On a watch page the panel offers **Clip this video**. The
    transcript arrives as YouTube segments it, each line with its timestamp in bold, under
    the title and the description.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide about comments">
    **Include comments** takes up to three pages of top threads, about 60, and, under the 25 with the most
    replies, the first page of those replies. It is off by default: it adds a run of
    extra requests, made one at a time, and usually a bigger bundle.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send it to the bundler">
    **Send** pushes the clipping into an open [fileconcat.com](/) tab, opening one if there
    is none, where you get one bundle and a live token count before you paste anything.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="Where to get it">
  The extension is on the [Chrome Web
  Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fileconcat-clipper/nhjmkijlimliapgbidajgbkangddnlhn),
  open source. There is a page about what it does at [/clipper](/clipper) and a
  reference at [/docs/clipper](/docs/clipper).
</Callout>

## Many videos at once

One video at a time is the small version of this problem. The bigger one is a course, a
conference track or a back catalogue, where the whole point is to ask a question across
all of it.

A channel's **Videos** tab and a search results page are listings, so the panel lists
the visible video rows it recognizes, each with a tap target. Each one you tap is opened
and read on its own. Scrolling to load more rows updates the panel with no click,
because it re-reads the page when the number of video links on it changes.

A channel's **Playlists** tab is different and more useful than it looks. It lists
playlists rather than videos, and taking one row queues the videos it recognizes on the first page
YouTube returns for that playlist, skipping any already queued and stopping at the queue limit, filed under the playlist's name.

<Callout type="warn" title="Not every video has a transcript">
  A clipping needs captions to exist, whether the uploader wrote them or YouTube generated
  them. A video whose transcript comes back empty or unreadable says so on its own row
  in the panel rather than arriving empty, and the queue goes on to the next row unless
  it stops for a rate limit, a bot check or a run of failures.
</Callout>

## Send the transcript to ChatGPT

Once the clippings are in a bundle you have one file with every video labelled by title,
which is the shape a model can work across. Copy it and paste it into ChatGPT or Claude,
or set Format to Plain and attach the .txt, a type both list. The questions that were not
practical before become ordinary ones: where does this speaker contradict that one, which
of these twelve talks covers retries, summarise the argument in the third one and quote
the part it rests on.

The send extends the bundle rather than replacing it, so you can add the repository, the
docs or the thread about the same subject and ask across all of it at once. None of it is
uploaded to us on the way. The clipping is made in your browser and handed to an open FileConcat tab, or
a new one.
